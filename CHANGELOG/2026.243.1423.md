# 2026.243.1423 — chore: advance pod-cstream to the tag carrying WP5's idle lock

## Summary

Advances the composed `pod-cstream` pin from `v2026.243.0310` to `v2026.243.1416`, the tag
carrying WP5's idle lock.

The pin had sat at `0310` through three `pod-cstream` releases, so this brings three things, not
one:

| tag | what it carries |
|---|---|
| `v2026.243.0814` | `gst-plugin-pipewire`. Without it the streamer **aborts at startup**, and since the streamer *is* the Wayland parent that takes the whole session down — it presented as an 11-minute bed hang, not as a missing package. |
| `v2026.243.0916` | desktop audio ON, gated on **both** channels reaching the stream. The earlier version negotiated `input_MONO` and PipeWire linked `monitor_FL` only, dropping the right channel while every check stayed green. |
| `v2026.243.1416` | WP5: `CSTREAM_LOCK_AFTER=10` arms the idle lock, a check that the streamer pin actually took, and the streamer source fetched over codeload instead of anonymous git. |

So WP6's audio work reaches a consumer through published tags here for the **first time** — until
now it had only ever run under a local override.

A partial repin is worse than none (advancing one consumer in a stale tree multiplies skew), which
is why this goes to the current tag rather than to the WP5 one alone.

`attempts=3, elapsed=10.511s` against `CSTREAM_LOCK_AFTER=10` with a 5s retry interval is what
makes that a real assertion: the first attempts find nothing, a later one finds `hyprlock`. A lock
that was already running would have passed on attempt 1, and `cstream-lock-idle-only` passing
beforehand rules that out from the other side.
